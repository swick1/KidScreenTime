Why I built this

I'm the guy who made this. I have three kids, and like every parent I know, I've spent a lot of years fighting with them about screen time. Not the "how many hours" fight. The other one. The one where the game doesn't go their way, and things get loud, and the easiest thing in the world is to just take the device away and be done with it.

Taking it away works, in the moment. But it doesn't teach anything. My kids didn't learn how to lose gracefully because I confiscated a controller. They learned that when I'm around, this is the deal, and when I'm not, it's whatever they can get away with.

As an Engineer, I write code for industrial control systems. If it's too complicated, it breaks at 1AM. Simple systems are the ones people actually use, and the systems people actually use are the only ones that get real results. There are advanced settings for nerds like me, but a parental control tool nobody bothers to configure properly doesn't help anyone. I've tried to make it painless by asking a few questions when you install it, rather than assuming how you want it to work.

What I actually believe

Getting frustrated isn't the problem. Getting frustrated and having no practice handling it is the problem. Kids need practice getting frustrated:

  • Losing a match.
  • Dying right before a checkpoint.
  • Watching hours of progress disappear.
  • Then calming themselves down instead of exploding.

That's the skill this app is trying to help build. It only gets built through practice, not through avoidance.

Gaming already hands you a near-endless supply of practice moments, for free, on a schedule your kid sets themselves, all afternoon, every day. You don't have to manufacture the frustration. It's already there. What's usually missing is someone consistent enough to notice it and gently interrupt it, every single time, without getting tired of doing it, and without turning it into a fight.

And it's not just about gaming. The kid who learns to notice "I'm about to lose it" and take a breath instead of throwing a controller is building a muscle they'll use in a classroom, on a team, in a job someday. Practice handling frustration in one place carries over into every other place. That's the actual bet this app is making.

Really, I'm not selling screen time software. I'm selling fewer arguments. Nobody wakes up wanting better scheduling software. They wake up tired of fighting every evening. That's the actual problem this solves.

What I'm not doing

I don't want your personal information, and I don't want to know how you use your computer. There's no account, no login, no cloud. Everything runs and stays on your own machine.

This isn't spyware for reading your kid's messages or tracking what they type. It only listens for tone and loudness, and it never records or saves audio.

There's no auto-update, and it never phones home to check for one. If I put out a new version, it'll be a new download you choose to install, on your own schedule.

Questions

My antivirus flagged the installer. Is this actually safe?
Almost certainly a false positive — this app has zero network code and its keyboard hook can't see what's typed (see the security page above for specifics). New, unsigned, self-relaunching software trips heuristic scanners by pattern, not by content.
Will this install on a work or school-managed computer?
Maybe not, and that's expected, not a bug — it's built to make itself hard for a kid to shut off, which looks the same to IT policy as something you shouldn't install.
Can I still get into my own computer?
Yes. There's an override password set up front, so you're always in control. It's built to resist a kid casually clicking past it, not to lock you out.
What if I want to stop using it?
Uninstall it like any other program. Nothing phones home, nothing is left watching in the background, and your config isn't uploaded anywhere to begin with.
